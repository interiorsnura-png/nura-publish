# Six-Week and Twelve-Week Kitchen Programmes: Planning Your Renovation

A kitchen programme needs to work with the rest of your renovation and the way your household will live through it. A shorter manufacturing option may be useful, but only if the surrounding decisions and site works can support it. A standard option may give more planning room without changing the design you want.

Nura has described an indicative twelve-week standard route and a paid six-week express option. For either one, ask for the dates, included stages and release conditions to be confirmed for your project before treating the headline as a completion promise.

## Establish what each window includes

Begin by asking what starts the clock and what ends it. Does the quoted period refer to a production stage, readiness for dispatch, delivery or another agreed milestone? Then identify any design, sampling, transport and installation work that sits outside that window.

Do not assume that the same wording means the same thing in two quotations. A useful programme connects the defined supply milestone to a practical plan for the home. That means including the stages that make the kitchen usable, rather than simply knowing when a cabinet is expected to leave a workshop.

## Assess how resolved the brief is

An accelerated route is easier to assess when the measured room, appliance selections, drawings and finish approvals are well advanced. Open structural information or a sample still under discussion may affect whether the proposed dates are realistic.

List the outstanding decisions and ask who will provide each answer. If a detail is likely to change, discuss its effect before committing to a production arrangement. Choosing an express option should not require pretending that unfinished information is already settled. It should be a response to a programme the team can reasonably coordinate.

## Compare the express fee with the wider benefit

Ask what the additional express cost covers and which scope it applies to. Then consider whether reaching that supply milestone earlier actually helps the renovation. If another critical stage will not be ready, faster cabinet production may offer little practical advantage.

A hypothetical homeowner might have an approved design and a prepared room, but a delayed appliance that controls a later connection. Another project might have all dependencies ready and benefit directly from an earlier cabinet delivery. The fee should be considered in the context of those different circumstances, without assuming that express is always the better route.

## Coordinate the building work and access

Ask the appointed coordinator to connect the kitchen programme with finished floor levels, service information, suitable site conditions and access. Agree what needs to be ready before delivery and fitting, and how the team will confirm that readiness.

If your own builder or installer is carrying out part of the work, record the handover responsibilities. Storage, receipt, protection and installation information need owners just as much as design approvals do. A shorter supply window does not automatically shorten the preparation required elsewhere on the site.

## Allow for the worktop sequence

Confirm the selected worktop supplier’s process. A stone template will commonly follow installation and checking of the cabinetry, with fabrication and fitting scheduled afterwards. Material selection and procurement can be discussed earlier, but the final sequence needs to suit the actual worktop and site.

Ask for the expected interval between those stages rather than using a generic estimate. The household needs to know when it will have a useful preparation surface and which connections depend on the worktop. Include that information in the wider programme whichever cabinet route you choose.

## Plan household disruption separately

A production period is not the same as the period without a working kitchen. The timing of removal, building work, fitting and connections determines the disruption inside the home. Ask whether the existing kitchen can remain in use for part of the process and what a temporary arrangement could involve.

Discuss any temporary services or equipment with the relevant professionals. Keep access and everyday needs in mind, particularly if the household includes children or anyone with specific mobility requirements. A realistic living plan can be as valuable as a faster supply date.

## Agree how changes and delays are handled

Ask what happens if you change an appliance, approve a new finish or discover a site condition after the programme is agreed. Record who assesses the effect, how a revised date is communicated and which tasks can continue while the issue is resolved.

Avoid treating either programme as immune to change. Useful planning identifies the dependencies and gives the household a clear contact when something moves. Keep the current drawings, approvals and programme together so that everyone is working from the same information.

## Choose the route that fits the whole project

The practical decision is whether the proposed route supports a complete, coordinated renovation. Compare the confirmed scope, express cost, readiness and surrounding works rather than choosing from the shortest number alone.
