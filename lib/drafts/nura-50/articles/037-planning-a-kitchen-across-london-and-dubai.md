# Planning a Kitchen Across London and Dubai

Commissioning a bespoke kitchen when your life spans two cities is genuinely different from a single-location project. The brief, the samples, the authority approvals and the site itself may all exist in different time zones, and the decisions that feel straightforward in a London studio can become complicated when your Dubai apartment is still under fit-out. Understanding where the complexity lives, and planning for it early, makes the difference between a project that flows and one that stalls at every handover point.

## Starting with One Coherent Brief

The most useful thing you can do before any drawings are produced is to consolidate your thinking into a single written brief, even if that brief is assembled across video calls and shared documents. When two households are involved, or when a client and their architect are working from different cities, small misalignments in the brief tend to surface as expensive drawing revisions later. Agreeing on the functional priorities, storage hierarchy, appliance preferences, worktop material direction, before the first sketch saves time on both sides of the project.

## Drawing Revisions and How They Are Managed

Ask how drawing development and revisions are included in the proposed design appointment. When a project spans London and Dubai, it is worth clarifying at the outset how revisions are submitted, how feedback is consolidated, and what constitutes a revision versus a new design direction. Sending fragmented feedback from multiple stakeholders across different time zones can inadvertently consume revision rounds quickly. Nominating a single point of contact on the client side, even informally, tends to keep the process cleaner for everyone.

## Samples Across Two Locations

Physical samples matter enormously in kitchen design. Timber veneer, lacquer colour, stone and hardware all read differently under different light conditions, and a finish that looks warm in a north-facing London kitchen may read quite differently under the stronger ambient light typical of Dubai interiors. Where feasible, requesting duplicate sample sets for both locations is worth discussing with your designer early. Colour and stain matching is handled within an agreed scope; where a finish requires specialist matching beyond that scope, this would typically be discussed and agreed separately rather than assumed as a standard inclusion.

## Scheduled Reviews and Time Zone Coordination

Building a rhythm of scheduled reviews into the project programme, rather than relying on ad hoc communication, tends to reduce the friction that accumulates across time zones. A fortnightly video call with a shared agenda and a running decision log gives both the design team and the client a clear record of what has been agreed and what remains open. This is particularly useful when authority approvals, such as building management sign-off in a Dubai development, introduce their own timelines that sit outside the kitchen manufacturer's control.

## Authority Approvals and Local Requirements

Dubai residential developments often require fit-out approval from a building management authority before work can begin on site. The scope, format and turnaround time of these approvals varies by development and can affect the overall project schedule in ways that are difficult to predict from London. It is worth establishing early whether your development requires drawings in a specific format, whether there are restrictions on appliance types or extraction routes, and who is responsible for submitting and tracking the approval. These are questions for your local project manager or fit-out contractor rather than your kitchen designer, but the answers will shape the programme.

## The Role of a Local Representative

For overseas projects, having a trusted local representative on the ground, whether that is an interior designer, a project manager or a fit-out contractor, is genuinely valuable. This person can attend site when you cannot, receive and check deliveries, liaise with the building management office and flag issues before they become costly. Nura has experience working with clients on overseas projects and can discuss how design collaboration with your local professional team might work in practice. Meetings can be arranged in London or Dubai by prior arrangement; confirm availability and the intended meeting location when making the appointment.

## Specification Differences Between Locations

A kitchen specified for a London home and one for a Dubai apartment may differ in meaningful ways beyond aesthetics. Electrical specifications, socket configurations, appliance voltage requirements and extraction regulations can all vary. Worktop material choices may also be influenced by the ambient humidity and temperature conditions of the space. Discussing the intended location of the kitchen early in the brief allows the design team to flag any specification considerations that are relevant to your particular project, rather than discovering them at the drawing stage.

## Keeping the Project Moving

Dual-location projects have more moving parts, but they are manageable with the right structure in place. A clear brief, an agreed revision process, physical samples in both locations where possible, scheduled reviews and a reliable local representative on site together form a practical framework.
