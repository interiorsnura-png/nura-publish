# Comparing Kitchen Quotes: A Line-by-Line Checklist

Two kitchen quotations can differ substantially even when their drawings look similar. The difference may sit in the cabinet schedule, an appliance allowance, installation scope or a finish that has not yet been selected. Working through each quotation line by line helps you understand what is actually being offered. The aim is to put the proposals onto a common basis before deciding whether a price difference represents useful value, a changed specification or work that still needs a separate budget.

## Start With the Cabinet Count and Configuration

Before anything else, confirm that every quote is pricing the same number of cabinets in the same positions. A quote that reduces a run of tall units to base units, or omits an island entirely, will look attractively priced until you add the missing pieces back in. Ask each supplier to annotate their drawing with unit references that correspond to their pricing schedule, so you can cross-check cabinet by cabinet rather than relying on a single lump sum.

## Carcass Material and Construction

The carcass, the structural box behind every door, varies considerably between suppliers. Some use 18 mm plywood, which offers good rigidity; others use melamine-faced chipboard (MFC), which is a perfectly serviceable engineered board when specified correctly, though standard P2-grade chipboard is not moisture-resistant and should not be confused with moisture-resistant variants. Ask each supplier to state the board type, thickness, and grade in writing. A quote that simply says "quality carcass" is not giving you enough information to compare.

## Door, Finish, and Colour Specification

Door material, profile, and finish account for a significant share of the overall cost and vary enormously. Confirm whether prices include a single colour throughout or whether two-tone combinations attract a premium. Check whether colour-matching to an existing element, a piece of furniture, a tile, a paint reference, falls within the standard scope or requires a separate matching charge. At Nura Interiors, colour and stain matching within an agreed scope is included without a special matching surcharge, which is worth clarifying with any supplier you are comparing.

## Hardware: Brand, Grade, and What Is Actually Included

Hinges, drawer runners, and lift systems are not interchangeable commodities. A quote specifying soft-close hinges tells you very little without a brand name and load rating. Ask whether the hardware is from an established manufacturer, Blum and HiGold are examples of brands whose exact products should be identified, and confirm whether internal drawer boxes, pull-out bins, and any specified inserts are included or priced separately. Hardware omissions are one of the most common sources of post-quote surprise.

## Worktops, Upstands, and Templating

Some quotes include a worktop allowance; others exclude it entirely and expect you to source stone or solid surface independently. Where a worktop is included, check whether templating (the precise measuring visit that must happen after cabinets are fitted) and fabrication are covered, or whether those are additional. Stone templating cannot responsibly happen before cabinets are in place and level, so any timeline that suggests otherwise should be questioned. Upstands, splashbacks, and window boards are frequently omitted from headline figures.

## Five Cost Drivers Worth Isolating

Certain items reliably separate quotes that look similar on the surface. It is worth asking each supplier to confirm their position on all five:

1. **Access and parking**, London deliveries and installations often incur congestion-zone charges, parking suspensions, or crane/hoist fees for upper-floor flats. These may appear as allowances or be excluded entirely.
2. **Appliance supply and integration**, Is appliance supply included? If so, at what specification? If not, who coordinates cutouts, ventilation, and electrical positions?
3. **VAT**, Confirm whether all figures are inclusive or exclusive of VAT. Compare both proposals on the same tax-inclusive basis rather than comparing an exclusive figure with an inclusive one.
4. **Plinth and cornice**, Plinths, cornices, and light pelmets are sometimes priced separately. Where a 30 mm plinth is offered as an option (feasibility depending on the specific installation), confirm whether that is within the quoted figure.
5. **Change provisions**, What happens if you alter a unit size or switch a door profile after sign-off? Some suppliers include a defined number of amendments; others charge from the first revision. Understanding this before you commit can save a meaningful sum.

## Fitting, Programme, and Handover

Installation cost and programme are rarely comparable without scrutiny. Ask whether fitting is included, subcontracted, or excluded. Confirm the anticipated lead time from deposit to delivery, different suppliers may count design, production, dispatch and fitting differently, though start and end dates will depend on when your slot is confirmed and your site is ready. A quote with a shorter headline timeline may be assuming conditions your project cannot meet.

## Snagging, Aftercare, and What Happens Next

Finally, ask each supplier how snagging is handled. Is there a dedicated visit, and who attends? What is the process if a door needs adjustment six months after handover? The answers will not appear in a quote document, but they are part of what you are buying. A lower price from a supplier with no clear aftercare process may cost more in time and frustration than a higher quote from one with a structured handover.
