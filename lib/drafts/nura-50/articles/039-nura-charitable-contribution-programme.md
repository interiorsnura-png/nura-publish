# Nura's Charitable Contribution: How the Programme Works

A room designed around everyday life also belongs within a wider community. Nura has stated a commitment to direct one per cent of sales towards helping children facing difficulties in the UK and internationally. The value of that intention depends on explaining it clearly and keeping the contribution accountable.

For a client, a charitable commitment should sit alongside a well-specified kitchen, rather than replace questions about the work itself. Here are the details worth understanding when a studio describes a percentage-of-sales programme.

## Start with the stated commitment

Nura’s stated intention is to connect its sales with support for children. That describes the purpose and the headline percentage, but a useful programme explanation also needs to define the basis of the contribution. A short phrase on a website cannot answer every accounting or timing question.

If this part of the studio’s work matters to you, ask for the current programme information during your project conversation. It should be possible to understand the commitment without having to infer details from a photograph, a slogan or an informal description of another project.

## Understand what the percentage applies to

Ask how the relevant sale value is defined. The programme should explain which transactions are included, how changes or cancellations are treated and whether tax or other amounts are part of the calculation. These are terms to establish, rather than assumptions that follow automatically from the word sales.

A contribution based on sales is also a different description from one based on profit. If a studio uses a percentage, the wording should remain consistent with the actual calculation. Clear definitions help the business keep its records and help clients understand the commitment being described.

## Ask when contributions are made

The event that starts a kitchen project may differ from the event used to calculate a charitable contribution. A deposit, a completed supply stage and a final invoice are different points in the programme. The contribution timing should be explained separately from the kitchen payment schedule.

Ask when amounts are calculated and transferred, and how any adjustments are handled. Do not assume a payment is made on your order date or at installation unless that is what the programme specifies. A clear timetable makes the intention easier to follow without turning every client project into a separate fundraising exercise.

## Understand how recipients are selected

A programme supporting children should be able to explain the type of organisation or work it aims to support and how recipients are considered. The practical detail may include a named organisation, a partnership or another documented route, depending on the arrangement actually in place.

Nura’s stated focus includes the UK and international needs. That does not identify a particular recipient or establish an individual impact result. Ask the studio about its current selection approach rather than assuming that a photograph or a geographic reference identifies where your project’s contribution will go.

## Look for proportionate reporting

Useful reporting connects the stated calculation with an amount contributed and an identified recipient, where appropriate. It does not need to make every project or client publicly identifiable. The relevant records should support whatever public statement the business chooses to make.

If totals or outcomes are published, distinguish the money contributed from the results reported by the recipient organisation. Neither should be invented or estimated for marketing copy. A measured account of the programme is more helpful than a dramatic number that cannot be explained or checked.

## Respect the people the programme supports

Supporting children does not require turning individual experiences into promotional content. Ask how the programme communicates its work respectfully and how any material supplied by a recipient organisation is approved for use. A kitchen client should not need personal details about a child to understand a contribution.

Images and stories need careful context. A general photograph of a family or a welcoming home is not evidence that the people shown have received support. Keeping those distinctions clear allows a business to describe its intentions without claiming a relationship or outcome that the image cannot establish.

## Keep the project decision grounded in the work

A client still needs a clear brief, suitable materials, a useful layout and agreed installation responsibilities. A charitable commitment should not obscure those decisions or make a vague quotation feel sufficient. Review the kitchen proposal on its own merits, then consider how the studio’s wider values relate to yours.
