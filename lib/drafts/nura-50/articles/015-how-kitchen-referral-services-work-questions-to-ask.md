# How Kitchen Referral Services Work: Questions to Ask Before Using One

Finding a kitchen supplier in a busy market can feel like a project in itself. Referral services promise to shortcut that search by connecting you with vetted companies, and for some homeowners they genuinely do. But the model varies considerably from one platform to the next, and understanding how a particular service earns its income, and who ultimately holds accountability for your project, is worth clarifying before you hand over your brief or your contact details.

## What a Kitchen Referral Service Actually Does

At its simplest, a referral service collects your requirements and passes them to one or more suppliers on its panel. Some platforms operate as straightforward directories; others run a more curated matching process. The common thread is that the service itself is rarely the party that designs, manufactures, or installs your kitchen. It sits between you and the companies that do. Understanding that distinction matters, because it shapes where accountability sits if something goes wrong.

## How These Services Typically Generate Revenue

Many referral platforms receive some form of compensation from the suppliers they recommend. This might be a flat listing fee, a fee per lead passed, or a payment triggered when a referral converts into a sale. The existence of a financial relationship between a platform and its panel members does not automatically make the recommendations unreliable, but it is worth knowing about. Ask directly whether the service receives any payment from suppliers and, if so, how that is structured. A transparent platform will answer clearly.

## Panel Breadth and How Suppliers Are Selected

A panel of three suppliers and a panel of thirty are very different propositions. Ask how many companies are on the panel, what criteria they must meet to join, and whether any suppliers are excluded for reasons other than quality, for instance, because they have not paid a listing fee. Some services refresh their panels regularly; others are more static. The breadth of the panel affects whether the match you receive reflects the best fit for your project or simply the available options within a particular commercial arrangement.

## Who Produces the Design and Who Owns It

Some referral journeys involve sharing a brief, a floor plan, or even preliminary drawings with the platform before you have agreed to work with any specific supplier. It is sensible to ask what happens to those documents. Are they shared with all panel members simultaneously? Are they stored, and if so for how long? Does the platform require your permission before passing design information to third parties? Your drawings represent intellectual effort and, in some cases, commercially sensitive information about your home. Clarify the privacy position before you share anything substantive.

## Who You Are Contracting With

This is perhaps the most important question of all. When you proceed with a supplier found through a referral service, ask which business you will contract with and what role, if any, the referral platform retains after introduction. Confirm this explicitly, and read the supplier's own terms carefully. Understanding the contract party matters for dispute resolution, for any deposit protection you might seek, and for knowing who to contact if timelines slip or specifications change.

## Who Manages Fitting and Local Support

Some referral services connect you with national suppliers who then subcontract installation to local tradespeople. Others work with regional companies that handle fitting in-house. Ask whether the company quoting your project will also be responsible for installation, or whether fitting is passed to a third party. If it is subcontracted, ask how the supplier oversees that work and who your point of contact is if a snagging issue arises after handover. A clear answer here is a reasonable expectation.

## Questions Worth Asking Any Referred Supplier Directly

Once you have a shortlist, treat each referred supplier as you would any other: ask for a detailed written specification, confirm lead times and what triggers the start of your project, and establish who manages your account day to day. If you are comparing multiple quotes, the guidance at [the kitchen quotation comparison guide](https://www.nura-interiors.com/post/how-to-compare-bespoke-kitchen-quotes-in-london) sets out a practical framework for making those comparisons on a like-for-like basis. Specification depth varies enormously between quotes, and a lower headline figure does not always reflect a lower-cost project once scope is aligned.

## When a Referral Service Adds Genuine Value

Referral platforms can be genuinely useful when you are unfamiliar with the supplier landscape, when you want to receive multiple quotes without conducting the research yourself, or when a service has done meaningful vetting that you would otherwise have to replicate. The value depends on the rigour of that vetting and the transparency of the commercial model. Used with clear eyes, a referral service is a tool, one that works best when you arrive with the right questions already prepared.
