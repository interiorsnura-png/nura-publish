# Bespoke Kitchen Payments: Deposits, Milestones and Finance Options

Commissioning a bespoke kitchen is one of the larger financial commitments most homeowners make in a decade, yet the payment structure rarely gets the same attention as the door profiles or worktop finish. Understanding what each stage of payment covers, and what it does not, protects both sides of the relationship and keeps a project moving without awkward surprises mid-build. This guide walks through the typical anatomy of a bespoke kitchen contract from first deposit to final sign-off, with honest notes on where flexibility exists and where it does not.

## What a Deposit Actually Covers

Ask what the deposit covers, which activities it authorises and how any cancellation or change would be handled under the actual terms. It may relate to design development, materials or a production commitment, but these should not be assumed to happen automatically on the day of payment. Read the appointment and ask for the relevant stages to be explained before paying. Questions about refunds or protection need to be answered for that agreement, rather than through a general statement about bespoke work.

## Milestone Payments and What Triggers Them

Most bespoke kitchen contracts split the balance across two or three further milestones rather than requesting everything upfront. Common trigger points include design sign-off, commencement of manufacture, delivery to site, and practical completion of installation. Each milestone should be tied to a verifiable event, not a calendar date alone, so that neither party is paying for work that has not yet happened or withholding payment for work that is genuinely complete. Ask your maker to list each milestone explicitly in the contract alongside the corresponding percentage or fixed sum.

## Design Changes After Sign-Off

Changes requested after design sign-off are one of the most common sources of budget creep on bespoke projects. Altering a cabinet run, swapping an appliance, or repositioning a socket after drawings are approved can require new cutting files, revised material orders, and additional design time. Reputable makers will price change requests transparently rather than absorbing them silently and recovering the cost elsewhere. Before signing off on drawings, take time to review them carefully, ideally with your architect or interior designer present, so that amendments remain minor rather than structural. You can read more about preparing for that review stage in our guide to [comparing bespoke kitchen quotes in London](https://www.nura-interiors.com/post/how-to-compare-bespoke-kitchen-quotes-in-london).

## Delivery and Fitting: Separate Contracts or One Agreement?

Some makers supply cabinets only; others supply and fit; others coordinate the entire project including stone, appliances, and electrical first-fix. Understanding which model applies to your project matters enormously for payment planning. If supply and installation are handled under separate contracts, for instance, a joinery supply contract and a separate fitting agreement with a third-party installer, each will carry its own deposit and milestone structure. Confirm this before you begin, because a gap between the two contracts can leave you exposed if one party delays and the other charges standing time.

## Retention: When It Applies and When It Does Not

If a retention or staged final balance is proposed, discuss it before agreeing the payment schedule. Establish the amount, the work or checks it relates to and the conditions for release. Do not assume that retention applies automatically or that one project’s arrangement determines another’s. The parties should be able to understand the sequence without relying on an informal conversation. Seek appropriate advice if the contractual terms are unclear; the practical aim is a documented payment process connected to the agreed work.

## Finance Options: What to Understand Before You Apply

If finance is being considered, identify the businesses involved and the role each plays. Ask who provides the credit agreement, who handles questions about repayments and how any payment to the kitchen supplier connects to project milestones. Do not infer lender or broker status from a showroom conversation. The [FCA’s consumer guidance](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/loan-fee-fraud) explains the importance of checking the identity and permissions of a financial firm. Read the provider’s actual terms before deciding whether an arrangement suits your circumstances.

## Borrowing Readiness: A Practical Checklist

If you are considering finance, it is worth assessing your position before approaching a lender. Useful questions to ask yourself include: Is the full project scope, including appliances, stone, and installation, costed, or only the cabinetry? Do you have a project contingency appropriate to the unresolved work for unforeseen site conditions? Is your income position stable enough to service repayments across the loan term? These are personal financial questions that only you can answer, and no kitchen studio should be advising you on them. Treat finance as a tool that enables a project rather than one that defines its scope.

## Protecting Yourself Through Clear Documentation

The single most effective protection on any bespoke commission is a written contract that both parties have read and understood. It should specify the scope of supply, the payment schedule with triggers, the process for change requests, the lead time (noting that standard and expedited timescales may differ), and the procedure for resolving disputes.
