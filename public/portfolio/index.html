WESTOVER

Wandsworth, London

This project was developed in close collaboration with the client, centred around a much-loved standalone piece of furniture that became the starting point for the entire design. Rather than treating the kitchen as a separate element, the intention was to reinterpret and extend the character of this existing piece into a cohesive architectural language.

Westover10 , 11

The original furniture featured fluted timber detailing and rich green tones, which informed the material palette. Dark smoked oak veneer was carefully detailed to echo the same proportions and rhythm, creating a sense of continuity throughout the space. This was paired with Verde Persia marble, introducing depth, texture, and a strong visual identity. To balance the richness of these materials, antique patinated brass elements were introduced across key junctions, door details, and accents.

These additions bring warmth and contrast, subtly referencing a more relaxed, layered aesthetic while elevating the overall composition. Given the intensity of the green marble, its application was carefully controlled. Carrara marble was introduced along the hob run to provide visual relief and contrast, allowing the Verde Persia to remain a focal feature rather than overwhelming the space.

Brass inlays within the Carrara splashback further tie the palette together, ensuring continuity across different materials. A bespoke stone sink, formed from the same Verde Persia marble, anchors the design and reinforces the monolithic quality of the material. Every element is considered in relation to the next, resulting in a kitchen that feels curated, balanced, and highly individual. This project reflects Nura’s approach designing beyond the conventional kitchen, creating spaces that are deeply

personal, materially rich, and built to stand the test of time. All timber elements were carefully book-matched to ensure continuity of grain across the cabinetry, creating a cohesive and uninterrupted visual flow. This attention to detail extends internally, where matching wood veneer is used within the drawer systems, enriching the overall material experience and reinforcing the integrity of the design.

Westover12 , 13

The client, a writer, required a space that would support long periods of daily use. In response, the kitchen was designed not only for cooking, but as a place to spend time comfortably and productively. A dedicated bar and coffee station was integrated into the layout, allowing the space to function throughout the

day beyond its primary use. Pocket doors were introduced to conceal this area when not in use, enabling the client to maintain a clean and ordered environment with minimal effort. This approach allows for flexibility, open when active, discreet when not.

Westover14 , 15
Westover16 , 17

Design decisions are guided by how a space will be used, but more importantly, by how it will be lived in over time. At Nura, we approach each project as a narrative, one that is shaped by the client’s routines, habits, and the subtle rhythms of daily life. The kitchen is not treated as a static object, but as an evolving environment that must respond intuitively to its user.

This perspective shifts the role of design from purely aesthetic composition to something more architectural and experiential. Circulation, sight lines, proportions, and adjacencies are all carefully considered to ensure that movement through the space feels natural and uninterrupted. Every element is positioned with intent, allowing the kitchen to function seamlessly within the wider context of the home.

Westover18 , 19

Materiality plays a key role in reinforcing this experience. Finishes are selected not only for their visual qualities, but for how they age, how they are touched, and how they contribute to the atmosphere of the space over time. The layering of materials, light, and detail creates depth, allowing the kitchen to feel both grounded and refined. Equally, flexibility is embedded into the design. Spaces are created to adapt to open up, to conceal, to transition between moments of activity and stillness. This ensures the kitchen remains relevant throughout the day, supporting different modes of use without compromising its clarity. By placing ourselves in the client’s position, we design with empathy and precision. We consider how mornings begin, how evenings unfold, and how small, everyday actions shape the overall experience of the space. The result is not simply a kitchen, but a carefully resolved environment that supports living in a meaningful and enduring way.

ELM PARK ROAD

Chelsea, London

A compact footprint demanded careful planning. The kitchen responds to the architecture and to the client's daily routines, resolving storage, circulation and material junctions with precision.

Elm Park Road22 , 23

The Elm Park Road project presented a unique spatial challenge, requiring a careful balance between functionality and visual clarity within a compact open plan setting. The kitchen needed to accommodate all the requirements of daily use while maintaining a calm and considered presence within the wider living space.

To address this, a full-height, floor-to-ceiling backdrop was introduced, maximising vertical storage and consolidating key functions into a single, efficient wall. This approach allowed the rest of the space to remain open and uncluttered, while significantly increasing usable storage capacity.

Elm Park Road24 , 25

A key constraint was the limitation of space typically required for handless systems. Standard designs rely on continuous grip channels, which would have reduced internal capacity. Instead, handles were integrated directly into the doors through a carved detail, maintaining a clean, handless appearance while optimising every millimetre. This solution not only resolved a functional issue but also introduced a distinctive design feature that defines the kitchen’s identity. Material selection played an equally important role.

Natural oak veneer was used to bring warmth and texture; however, to avoid a traditional or overly heavy appearance, this was balanced with dark metallic lacquered wall units. The contrast creates depth and breaks the material palette, resulting in a composition that feels both contemporary and timeless. The result is a compact kitchen that performs efficiently while presenting a strong architectural presence demonstrating how spatial constraints can be transformed into defining design opportunities.

STABLE FARM

Gerrards Cross, Slough

Material contrast defines this kitchen: Nero Marquina and Calacatta Oro porcelain meet precisely detailed cabinetry and integrated lighting. Strong geometry and carefully resolved junctions create a composed, durable interior.

Stable Farm34 , 35

A generous space calls for a confident and deliberate design approach precisely what this kitchen embodies. Defined by scale and material clarity, the scheme is anchored by a striking full-height backdrop, where two contrasting porcelain slabs are applied across the cabinetry.

Each surface is carefully vein-matched and continuous across the tall run, creating a cohesive and uninterrupted composition. Subtle gold metallic accents are introduced to soften the intensity of the stone. At the centre, a sculptural island forms the focal point of the space.

Stable Farm36 , 37

Crafted as a monolithic volume in light-toned porcelain, its faceted geometry introduces a sense of movement and precision, contrasting with the linearity of the surrounding cabinetry. The island integrates cooking functions seamlessly, with a ceiling-mounted extraction feature positioned directly above, maintaining clean sight lines across the open space.

Functionality is carefully layered within the design. Behind the tall units, concealed storage and a dedicated appliance housing system provide a practical working area, allowing smaller appliances to be hidden when not in use. Internally, warm metallic finishes and integrated lighting enhance usability while reinforcing the overall material language of the kitchen.

TANSLEY FARM

Cotswold, Oxfordshire

The design is organised around proportion, movement and daily use. Calacatta Hermitage stone and restrained metal accents bring depth to the palette, while carefully detailed cabinetry supports a clear, functional plan.

Tansley Farm40 , 41

Tansley Farm42 , 43
Tansley Farm44 , 45

Achieving colour consistency in this porcelain stone requires careful selection across multiple slabs. Variations in tone, veining, and pattern are reviewed, matched, and planned prior to fabrication to ensure alignment with the overall design intent. Fabricating a full stone island is a complex process requiring precise templating, manufacturing, and installation.

Particular attention is given to joint lines, edge details, and vein direction to maintain continuity across all surfaces. The success of the final result relies on how seamlessly the stone integrates within the wider design. When executed correctly, it forms a strong, monolithic element that is both functional and visually resolved.

Tansley Farm46 , 47

A key aspect of this project reflects Nura’s approach to bespoke design and technical development. Regardless of project scale, we invest the same level of care and precision into every detail, often developing custom solutions and prototypes to meet specific client and designer requirements. For this kitchen, the client requested fully clad doors in porcelain, a detail that required a considered technical response. To achieve this, an anodised aluminium subframe was engineered to provide structural stability while remaining lightweight.

This ensures long-term durability, reducing strain on hinges and drawer mechanisms while maintaining precise alignment over time. The porcelain, at 6mm thickness, was then applied to this framework, allowing for a refined, monolithic appearance without compromising performance. To maintain visual continuity, the aluminium structure was lacquered to match the exact tone of the Calacatta Hermitage stone, ensuring a seamless integration between the internal framework and the external surface. This level of detail exemplifies Nura’s commitment to delivering bespoke solutions where design intent is supported by technical rigour, and every element is resolved to perform as well as it looks.

HARBOUR

Chelsea, London

The Minimal Cannetatto collection explores rhythm, shadow and tactility through fluted surfaces, precise metal detailing and a restrained material palette.

Harbour50 , 51

At Harbour, this project marked a defining moment in the evolution of Nura’s design language. It was here that we first introduced our ribbed veneer doors with a square- edge profile a deliberate reinterpretation of the traditional fluted form. By removing the softer, rounded edges typically associated with fluting, the detailing becomes sharper, more architectural, and inherently contemporary. This approach modernises a classic motif, giving it greater clarity and precision while maintaining the depth and rhythm that fluted surfaces naturally provide. The result is a refined surface language that has since become a signature element within Nura’s collections. The material palette is carefully composed to reinforce warmth and tactility.

Ribbed wood veneer is paired with metallic lacquered finishes in patinated bronze, introducing contrast and subtle reflectivity. This is complemented by Taj Mahal stone, whose soft veining and tonal variation bring balance and lightness to the overall composition. Together, these materials create a layered and cohesive environment one that feels warm, intimate, and timeless. The combination of texture, tone, and detail allows the kitchen to sit comfortably within its setting, offering both visual richness and everyday usability. This project reflects Nura’s ability to evolve familiar design elements into something distinctive, establishing a language that is both contemporary and enduring.

The result is a practical, resolved kitchen that respects both the Victorian context and the client's priorities.

Bespoke Joinery68 , 69

At Nura, our scope extends beyond kitchens into a wider offering of bespoke joinery. From wardrobes and media units to utility spaces, larders, and boot rooms, we design and deliver fully integrated solutions that form part of a cohesive interior language. This approach allows us to maintain consistency in both detailing and materiality across an entire project. By carrying key design elements such as finishes, profiles, and hardware throughout different spaces, we create a unified and considered environment rather than a series of disconnected elements.

Beyond aesthetics, this integrated service brings practical value. Clients and contractors benefit from working with a single specialist responsible for the full second-fix joinery package, ensuring clarity, efficiency, and a consistent level of quality from concept through to installation. The result is a seamless extension of our design philosophy across the home. As shown here, a standalone joinery piece within the reception area reflects the same material palette, handle detailing, and craftsmanship as the kitchen, reinforcing a coherent and well-resolved interior.

Lillie Square70 , 71

At Lillie Square, this bespoke media unit was designed to achieve a balance between classic proportions and contemporary restraint. The brief called for a piece that felt timeless and elegant, without dominating the room. The result is a carefully composed joinery element that integrates seamlessly within the space while maintaining a quiet presence.

Particular attention was given to the client’s personal collection, with each shelf and compartment designed around specific objects. Proportions, spacing, and alignment were all considered to ensure that every item is both accommodated and presented with intention, transforming the unit into a curated display as well as a functional piece of furniture.

Akehurst Joinery76 , 77

A primary challenge on this project was to maximise storage while maintaining clear and comfortable circulation throughout the space.

Internally, the use of darker timber adds warmth and richness, enhancing the experience of the joinery when in use. This contrast between exterior and interior surfaces creates a layered and considered composition.
